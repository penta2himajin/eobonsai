# GEMV roofline closure: DRAM is saturated, there is no kernel-side headroom
# Measured 2026-09-27/28.

## The ceiling is not 300 GB/s for this access geometry

tools/microbench/gemv.cu, bench_row_stream(): pure sequential read of one row per block,
blocks striding across rows, at the real PQ2_0 row sizes.

  row = 1360 B  (K=5120)   272.2 GB/s
  row = 4640 B  (K=17408)  274.8 GB/s
  row = 16384 B            274.5 GB/s
  row = 256 B              154.3 GB/s

The streaming ceiling is ~275 GB/s and is independent of row length above ~1 KB. The
earlier 298-307 GB/s figure came from a single large contiguous buffer, which is not the
geometry the model presents.

## GEMV already meets or beats it

ncu, served shape (ncols_dst=1), 6 launches, results/ncu-served-gemv.csv:

  1024   10.8   184.4    53.1   14.0   97.3   53.1   59.4   56

 17408  174.7   277.7    79.3    3.2   95.8   75.6   71.9   48

The best shape (17408 rows, the ffn_gate/up weights) reads 277.7 GB/s, which is 101% of the
measured streaming ceiling for this geometry. L1 hit rate is 96-98%, so the loads are being
served from L1 and the misses go to DRAM; L2 hit rate is 3-14%, i.e. almost everything
misses L2 and streams from DRAM, which is exactly what a bandwidth-bound streaming kernel
should look like.

## Conclusion

The decode GEMV is DRAM-bandwidth saturated. There is no kernel-side optimization left:
the arithmetic is already dp4a, the loads hit L1, occupancy is 59-72% with 56 registers,
and the fastest shape exceeds the measured pure-streaming ceiling for the same geometry.

The apparent "24% headroom" was an artifact of comparing against the wrong ceiling
(300 GB/s from a contiguous buffer) rather than the applicable one (275 GB/s for
row-structured reads). Measured against the right ceiling the gap is ~0% for the dominant
shape and reflects kernel launch and reduction overhead on the small shapes.

Step B of the optimization objective is therefore closed by measurement, not by exhausting
attempts: the target does not exist.
